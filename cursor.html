OpenAI Ends Cursor Access After SpaceX Deal, Promises To Support The People It Just Unplugged

San Francisco — Aug. 28, 2026

OpenAI announced Friday that it is ending its partnership with the coding editor Cursor following Cursor’s acquisition by SpaceX, a development the company said would most affect the developers who use Cursor, which several of those developers had already noticed.

Under the proposal, Cursor’s direct access to OpenAI models would end November 12. Officials said they were ready to go above and beyond to support users through the transition. Asked what “beyond” included, a spokesperson pointed back to the sentence about going above and beyond.

A senior engineer in Austin said he understood the decision as a matter of corporate strategy and did not understand it as a matter of the function he was in the middle of writing. “I asked Cursor for a migration plan,” he said. “It started one. Then the status bar asked if I had considered a different model family, a different editor, or a different career.”

OpenAI said it cares deeply about the developer experience. Developers said they also cared about the developer experience, and that until Friday they had been having one.

Cursor, now a SpaceX property, said it would keep building software, only now with access to an unusually large number of GPUs and a launch calendar. One user reported that autocomplete had begun suggesting “T-minus” in places where a variable name used to go. Another said the editor offered to refactor his monolith and also his life.

SpaceX did not immediately comment. A rocket on a pad in Texas also did not comment, though it was already pointed the usual direction.
